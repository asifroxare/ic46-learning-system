Concept 01 • Core Definition

Accounting vs. Bookkeeping

Bookkeeping is the recording groundwork (primary/subsidiary books). Accounting is the complete art of identifying, recording, classifying, summarising, analysing, and communicating financial results (AICPA).

Rule: "All transactions are events, but all events are not transactions."
Concept 02 • Core Objectives

Position vs. Performance

Financial accounting serves two prime objectives:

  • Performance: Ascertained by Revenue Accounts and Profit & Loss Account.
  • Financial Position: Ascertained by the Balance Sheet (Solvency & Liquidity).
Concept 03 • Insurance Specifics

Solvency: ASM vs. RSM

Insurance health places maximum emphasis on Solvency and Liquidity:

  • ASM (Available Solvency Margin): Excess of admissible assets over liabilities (from Balance Sheet).
  • RSM (Required Solvency Margin): Computed based on premium volume and incurred claims.
Concept 04 • Inherent Limitations

7 Key Limitations of Accounting

  • Permits alternative treatments (e.g., FIFO vs. Weighted Average).
  • Influenced by personal judgments (e.g., loss surveyor claim estimates).
  • Ignores non-monetary factors (staff loyalty, skills).
  • Historical cost ignores current realisable market value.
  • Does not classify direct/indirect or variable costs.
Concept 05 • Statutory Retention

Sec. 128 Companies Act 2013

Books of account must be maintained on accrual basis under the double entry system. All books and vouchers must be preserved in good order for a minimum of 8 financial years.

Concept 06 • Operational Registers

17 General Insurance Registers

Operating offices maintain specific registers: Premium Register, Refund Premium Register, Claims Intimation Register, Claims Recovery Register, Salvage Register, Cover Note Control Register, Co-insurance Register, and Dishonoured Cheques Register.
